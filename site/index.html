Check your message before you send it

Paste an email, text or comment. An AI model reads it and flags anything that could come across as rude, insulting, profane or threatening, sentence by sentence. It runs entirely in your browser: nothing you type leaves your device.

Checks automatically as you type.

How it works

  1. The model. toxic-bert (Unitary's BERT model, trained on about 160,000 Wikipedia comments labeled by people) scores text on six kinds of harmful language. This copy (Xenova/toxic-bert, 8-bit, about 110 MB) is converted to run in the browser.
  2. In your browser. transformers.js runs the model with WebAssembly. The model files are part of this site, so after the first load the checker works offline and nothing is sent anywhere.
  3. Sentence by sentence. The whole message gets an overall score, and each sentence is scored on its own, so you can see exactly which line to rewrite.
  4. The verdict. Below 30% on every category: looks fine. 30–70%: might come across badly. Above 70%: likely to offend.
  5. Limits. It catches rude, insulting, profane, threatening and hateful language. It does not catch politely cold or passive-aggressive tone ("Per my last email…"), and it can flag friendly swearing ("that was damn good").
